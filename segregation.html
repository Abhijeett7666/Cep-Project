Waste Segregation Guide

Learn how to separate waste at source to support community hygiene and circular recycling.

Municipal Notice & Variance Disclaimer

Not all materials are universally recyclable in every region. Collection frequency, bin color coding, and processing facilities vary by municipality and city council. Always verify and comply with your local municipal guidelines.

Wet Waste (Biodegradable)

Organic & Compostable
Definition

Organic waste originating from plant or animal sources that breaks down naturally through biological decomposition into rich organic matter.

Common Examples
  • Kitchen food scraps, fruit rinds, and vegetable peels
  • Leftover cooked meals, bread, and eggshells
  • Used tea leaves, coffee grounds, and nutshells
  • Garden clippings, dried leaves, and wilted flowers
Handling & Disposal Method

Collect inside a dedicated green bin. Drain excess water and gravies before disposal to prevent noxious odors and liquid seepage. Use for household composting or community organic waste converters.

Safety Precautions

Always keep bins covered to avoid pest attraction. Never mix plastics, glass, or pet feces into organic compostable waste.

Local Municipal Guideline: Municipal collection schedules vary. Many civic bodies collect wet waste daily. Always verify your local municipality's green bin pickup rules.

Dry Recyclable Waste

Recyclable
Definition

Clean, non-biodegradable materials that can be sorted, shredded, or melted down and re-manufactured into new commercial goods.

Common Examples
  • Newspapers, notebooks, cardboard boxes, and carton packaging
  • Rinsed plastic beverage bottles, food containers, and jugs
  • Glass bottles and clean sauce jars
  • Tin cans, beverage cans, aluminum foil, and metal caps
Handling & Disposal Method

Clean and air-dry containers before binning to eliminate food contamination. Flatten cardboard cartons and crush beverage bottles to conserve bin volume. Keep inside a blue bin.

Safety Precautions

Wrap shattered glass bottles or sharp metal lids inside thick paper before disposal to safeguard waste handlers from cuts.

Local Municipal Guideline: Not all plastics are universally recyclable. Recycling eligibility depends on local Material Recovery Facility (MRF) infrastructure.

E-Waste (Electronic Waste)

Special Recovery
Definition

Discarded electrical and electronic appliances, components, and gadgets that have reached the end of their functional utility.

Common Examples
  • Smartphones, chargers, cables, and power adapters
  • Laptops, computer peripherals, and keyboards
  • Dry cell batteries, lithium-ion battery packs, and button cells
  • CFL bulbs, LED tubelights, and printed circuit boards (PCBs)
Handling & Disposal Method

Store separately in a clean, moisture-free container. Never dispose of e-waste in general household trash or set it on fire. Hand it over to certified e-waste drives or authorized collection kiosks.

Safety Precautions

Never dismantle batteries or circuit boards manually. Punctured batteries can ignite or leach toxic heavy metals (lead, cadmium, mercury) into soil and water.

Local Municipal Guideline: E-waste requires specialized recovery centers to neutralize heavy metals and recover precious elements. Consult your city portal for authorized collection drives.

Hazardous Household Waste

Toxic & Regulated
Definition

Substances posing substantial hazards to human safety and ecosystems due to flammable, toxic, corrosive, or chemically reactive characteristics.

Common Examples
  • Paints, synthetic varnishes, and enamel thinners
  • Pesticides, weedicides, and rodent control pellets
  • Acidic bathroom cleaners, drain unblockers, and ammonia solutions
  • Motor oil, brake fluids, and lead-acid vehicle batteries
Handling & Disposal Method

Retain products in their original labeled containers whenever feasible. Store in a secure, ventilated spot away from heat, open flames, children, and domestic pets.

Safety Precautions

Always wear protective gloves and a face covering during handling. NEVER pour hazardous liquids into sinks, toilets, or street gutters, as they severely poison municipal water bodies.

Local Municipal Guideline: Hazardous waste disposal is strictly governed by environmental protection laws. Surrender only at designated municipal hazardous collection drop-off points.

Special Waste (Sanitary & Bulky)

Biomedical & Bulky
Definition

Waste items requiring specialized bio-secure disposal or dedicated oversized transport due to hygiene considerations or large volume.

Common Examples
  • Sanitary napkins, baby diapers, and incontinence products
  • Used medical bandages, antiseptic cotton swabs, and surgical masks
  • Discarded mattresses, large broken furniture, and bed frames
  • Renovation rubble, broken tiles, and construction debris
Handling & Disposal Method

Wrap sanitary hygiene items securely in newspaper or biodegradable bags, marked with a distinct red dot for sanitation worker safety. Contact local civic helplines to schedule dedicated pickup for bulky furniture.

Safety Precautions

NEVER flush diapers or sanitary napkins down toilets; they do not dissolve and cause catastrophic sewer clogs. Protect sanitation workers from direct exposure to biological waste.

Local Municipal Guideline: Sanitary waste is treated in high-temperature municipal incinerators. Bulky items require scheduled municipal on-demand transport.

Wet Waste (Biodegradable)

Organic waste originating from plant or animal sources that breaks down naturally through biological decomposition into rich organic matter.

Common Items:

Food scraps, vegetable peels, tea leaves, fruit rinds, stale cooked food, eggshells, and garden clippings.

Disposal & Safety:

Use a green covered dustbin. Drain liquid before disposal. Ideal for kitchen composting.

Warning: Keep covered to avoid flies and rodents. Never mix plastics.

Dry Recyclable Waste

Clean, non-biodegradable items that can be processed and remanufactured into new products.

Common Items:

Paper, cardboard boxes, clean plastic bottles, beverage cartons, glass containers, and metal tins.

Disposal & Safety:

Rinse and dry containers. Flatten cardboard to save space. Collect in blue bins.

Warning: Wrap broken glass in thick paper to protect sanitation workers.

E-Waste (Electronic Waste)

Discarded electrical and electronic gadgets and components that reached end-of-life.

Common Items:

Old smartphones, chargers, dead lithium batteries, cables, power banks, computer mice, and tubes.

Disposal & Safety:

Deposit at certified e-waste drives or authorized drop kiosks. Never burn or dump with general trash.

Warning: Never dismantle batteries manually; punctured batteries leach heavy metals and catch fire.

Hazardous Household Waste

Substances posing toxic, flammable, or corrosive hazards to human health and ecosystems.

Common Items:

Paints, thinners, garden pesticides, rodent poisons, toilet cleaners, and motor oil.

Disposal & Safety:

Retain in original labeled containers. Dispose of exclusively at municipal hazardous waste drop-offs.

Warning: NEVER pour down sinks or street drains; it poisons municipal waterways.

Special Waste (Sanitary & Bulky)

Items requiring bio-secure handling or oversized transportation due to hygiene or volume.

Common Items:

Sanitary pads, diapers, medical bandages, discarded mattresses, and renovation debris.

Disposal & Safety:

Wrap sanitary waste in newspaper marked with a red dot. Schedule on-demand bulk pickup for furniture.

Warning: NEVER flush sanitary pads down toilets; they cause severe sewer clogs.
